4 · Colour word, and two people at onceThe word being said turns gold. When two people speak over each other, each gets their own line with their own name, up to two lines. A third voice waits for a line to clear.

Written down, a few seconds at a timeEach device sends its own short piece of sound. Only the sound, and it isn't kept. The words come back to that device.
→
On the programme, with the nameThe room shows "TUNDE BAKARE: …" on every screen. The name comes from who sent it, never from guessing voices.
  • Voices are never mixed, because no device ever hears two people's microphones.
  • Nobody is subtitled without knowing: each speaker sees a notice and can turn their own off.
  • The editor stays in charge: clear the line, switch a speaker off, or type a line by hand.
  • Subtitles can be wrong and go out unchecked, so the room's content rules and the moderators' tools still apply.

5 · Several speakers: who writes whatEach person on stage has their own device write down their own voice. The name on a line is the account that sent it, so two voices are never run together and nobody is given words they didn't say.
